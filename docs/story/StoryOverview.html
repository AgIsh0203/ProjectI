Squirrel Wheels

The game in a nutshell

Squirrel Wheels is a silly driving game for 2 to 4 friends playing together online. Each player is a tiny squirrel, and together they drive one falling-apart jeep: one squirrel steers, one works the pedals, and the rest run around fixing whatever just broke. Squirrels keep falling out. The jeep keeps catching fire. That's the fun.

The squirrels never say a word. The story is told by the voices on the jeep's radio, and by whatever goes wrong on the road. It's made for laughing with friends, and for watching someone else's crew fall apart on a stream.

How it begins

The game opens on three lines of text:

The squirrels of the Old Oak were sick of plain acorns.
So they snuck onto an Acornucopia truck for a taste of the golden ones.
The truck didn't stop for three days.

The crew

The players are a crew of squirrels from the Old Oak, a huge old tree in Maple Park, a city park far down the valley. They aren't hungry. They're bored: plain acorns, every day, every winter. They promised the whole colony a feast (“this winter, we eat like kings”), so they went after the most famous acorns in the country. Acornucopia's golden acorns: “fine glazed acorns since 1962”.

The squirrels have no names in the story. Each player gets a fur colour (orange, blue, pink or lime), and that's how the voices on the radio tell them apart: “The orange one's out of the jeep again.”

One more thing about golden acorns: they're too good. Every now and then a squirrel sneaks a taste and can't stop chewing, so all it can do is point and wave until it's finished. A voice on the radio has a theory: “They're eating the evidence.”

The voices on the radio

Dispatch. Works the radio desk at Acornucopia, tracking every truck by GPS. Dry, deadpan, very professional about very stupid things. She spends the whole week calling the truck's driver on a radio that is now bolted into a squirrel jeep.

Foreman Buck, of Buck & Sons. A beaver who runs a construction company: “demolition, earthworks, crane hire, the school run and snow clearing”. That means a lot of machines, which means a lot of machines for squirrels to borrow. Jittery, fast, panicky, and very protective of every one of them. Nobody thinks a beaver running a building firm is strange. Squirrels driving a jeep, though...

Gravy Train. A trucker on the same roads all week, and the first to spot the jeep. Big, warm and slow, loves radio gossip, and becomes the crew's biggest fan.

Night Owl. A conspiracy caller who listens to the radio all night, and now all day too. Absolutely sure the squirrels are organising. Right about everything; nobody believes a word.

Unit Seven. The truck's driver. Has exactly one line, at the very end.

Two more characters never speak. TINY is the junkyard's enormous guard dog (it says TINY on the collar), who chases the jeep across the whole country. And there's the deer that caused the crash, who turns up wherever the crew stops for the night, and just watches.

Why the radio talks to them

The radio in the jeep is the truck's own radio, taken from the wreck. In the crash its talk button got stuck, so it broadcasts everything near the jeep: the engine, the crashes, the horn, the panting. That's how Dispatch hears the jeep without seeing it. Every crate of golden acorns also carries a GPS tag, so she always knows where her cargo is. She just doesn't know who's driving it.

The squirrels can hear everyone, but they can't answer. The jeep's little horn is their only voice.

Day 1: The Crash Site the free demo

Morning, in the mountains. The Acornucopia truck lies on its side in Rusty Bucket Salvage, a mountain junkyard, smoking. Its driver swerved for a deer, crashed through the fence and walked to town to find a tow truck. Crates of golden acorns lie everywhere. Chained by the gate, a huge dog barks at everything. At the edge of the trees, a deer watches.

The radio crackles with the morning weather: “First snow expected in six days.” Then Dispatch calls the driver: “Unit Seven, this is Dispatch. Your GPS says you've stopped. ...In a junkyard. Respond.” A trucker answers instead: the driver went by on foot, walking to town. “Looked fine. ...Looked sad.”

The crew can't drive a truck, so they build a jeep: a toy jeep from the junk pile, a car battery for an engine, a kitchen sponge for a brake, a cardboard door held on with tape, and a wobbly tyre that someone has to hold on. Over the radio, Dispatch hears all of it and understands none of it: “Unit Seven, I'm hearing an engine. ...That's not your engine. Your engine doesn't go ‘bzzt’.”

The moment the jeep rolls out with the golden acorns, two things happen. Dispatch sees her cargo move on the map: “Be advised: your cargo has left the truck. ...Your cargo is driving.” And the dog snaps its chain. It chases the jeep all the way to the gate before it runs out of breath. A voice on the radio whispers: “That bark sounded personal.”

The road home climbs over the mountain. At the first river, the trucker spots them: “Just got passed by a jeep with squirrels in it. And one of 'em was steerin' with its whole body.” Dispatch puts out an alert: “Be on the lookout for a small jeep. Possibly a toy. Driven by... I'm being told squirrels.”

The mountain throws everything at them: a cobbled lane that bounces acorns out of the back, a hill named Acorn Hill (“You think that's a coincidence?”), a fancy sports car broken down across a cliff road, and a fallen pine on a hairpin bend.

At the bridge, Foreman Buck's crew is taking the old railings off with a wrecking ball. Buck spots the jeep too late: “Why is there a jeep on my bridge? STOP THE BALL— it doesn't stop, it's a BALL!”

Then comes a rockslide (“Rockslide on Rocky Road. Yes, I know. That's why it's called that.”), a slalom between posts on the summit (“They named a ROAD after them. It's already happening.”), and jumps. Big jumps. “Unit Seven, your GPS says you were briefly... airborne. Please confirm. ...Please don't confirm.”

At the top of the long road down, the crew has a choice. The long way is Wiggly Road: six slow bends and, at a lookout, an Acornucopia picnic hamper full of golden acorns to make up for the ones that fell out (“...and now they've robbed a picnic.”). The short way is Buck's Cut, straight down the mountain, but it's closed. An old ranger station stands in the middle of the road, due to be knocked down on Thursday. Buck's digger is parked right next to it. With the keys in.

“No-no-no-no, don't touch the— that's my digger, that's a WORK digger— ...oh, they're touching it.” The crew parks the jeep and piles into the digger: one squirrel drives the tracks, one works the arm, one the bucket. They flatten the ranger station while Buck pleads over the radio: “At least use the BUCKET, not the cab! The CAB is where I eat!” When the dust settles, Buck is confused: “That's the job. That was Thursday's job. ...They did Thursday's job.”

The two roads meet again at Cobble Curve, and there, waiting, is the dog. It's huge. Its collar says TINY. The trucker shouts the only advice that works: “Somebody honk at it!” The jeep's horn is tiny, and it works anyway. TINY chases them all the way down the rocky descent, snapping at tyres and at any squirrel careless enough to be on foot. It only gives up at the river at the bottom. TINY doesn't like rivers. Yet.

At dusk the jeep reaches a campground. Nobody sleeps until the whole crew is in. The deer is there, watching.

And the radio crackles one last time: “Unit Seven, you've been quiet all day. ...Unit Seven, if that's you breathing, honk twice.”

If the crew honks twice: “...That was a coincidence. ...Honk three times.” If they honk three times: “...Okay. That's not Unit Seven. ...Who IS this?” If they don't honk at all, she sighs: “Great. I'm talking to a wrecked truck.” And if they lean on the horn: “That was a lot more than two. Are you okay in there?”

That's where the free demo ends, with a glimpse of the machines still to come and the crew's results for the day.

Day 2: The Washout

Overnight, a storm floods the farmland below the mountains. Dispatch's morning call is firm: “First snow expected in five days. ...And yesterday was a coincidence. I've decided.” If nobody honked last night, she tries again instead.

The road runs through muddy fields, flooded lanes and dripping fences. The storm has washed a sinkhole into the main road: one more job on Buck's list. The short way is to borrow Buck's sand truck, back it up to the hole and fill it. Buck, watching, has no idea how to feel: “They filled my sinkhole. That was on my list. Why are they doing my list?” The long way fords the flooded river, where the current sweeps away any squirrel who steps off the jeep. On a farmhouse porch along the way sits an Acornucopia doorstep delivery box. It doesn't stay there.

Gravy Train tells every trucker on the radio that the squirrel jeep is real. Night Owl reaches a new conclusion: “They crossed the flood. In a jeep. They have a navy now.” And TINY, who wouldn't cross a river yesterday, swims the whole flood after them.

They spend the night in a barn on a hill, high above the water. The deer is already there.

Day 3: The Old Quarry

Four days to the snow. The road crosses an old quarry: gravel pits, cliffs, rusting machines. The quick way across is cut by a gap in the rock, too wide for a jeep to jump. Dispatch starts testing the crew (“If anyone in that jeep can hear me: honk once for yes.”) and gives them her first tip, strictly hypothetically: there's a school bus parked by the pit.

The short way takes two of Buck's machines. First the sand truck again, to build a ramp up to the high ledge. Then the school bus, driven up the ramp and parked across the gap, so the jeep can drive over its roof. Buck has stopped being surprised: “...that's my bus too. Of course it is.” Then, less calmly: “The bus is not a bridge! The bus is for CHILDREN!” The long way winds all the way down into the pit and back up the other side on loose gravel, past the quarry workers' lunch cooler (Acornucopia snack packs, naturally).

Gravy Train starts a fan club (members so far: Gravy Train, and a cousin). Night Owl spots a pattern: “A ramp. And now a bus. They're building infrastructure.” TINY takes a shortcut down a gravel chute, on its belly.

They sleep in the quarry office.

Day 4: Town Outskirts

Three days to the snow. The road leaves the wild and runs into town: traffic, roundabouts, rail yards, and the Acornucopia factory, with a giant golden acorn on the roof. Right next door is Acornucopia's headquarters, and at one of its windows sits Dispatch. “...They're outside my window. ...Are they waving?” From that moment she's a fan: “Okay. I'm a fan. Nobody tell the client.”

A rail yard blocks the way. The short way: Buck's crane truck lifts the jeep clean over the rail lines (“I'm not even mad. Bring it back with a full tank.”). The long way: the level crossing, where a freight train takes forever to pass, unless the crew gambles on beating it. Next to the crossing is the Acornucopia outlet store. Dispatch logs the raid “as a promotion”.

Truckers on the ring road honk hello. Night Owl is horrified: “They're in the rail network.” TINY rides a passing freight train and leaps off in front of the jeep.

They spend the night at a gas station. “Their jeep runs on a battery. Why are they at a gas station?” The deer is at pump four.

Day 5: The Dam

First frost. Two days to the snow. The road crosses a huge dam, and the way over is cut by the spillway. Dispatch now helps openly: “Spillway opens in ninety seconds. ...I didn't tell you that.”

The short way needs two machines at once: the crane truck holds the school bus across the spillway while the jeep drives over it, with Buck on the radio, finally acting as the crew's foreman: “Swing left. LEFT. My left! ...Hold the bus! Go, go, go!” Afterwards: “That was beautiful. Buck and Sons has never done anything that beautiful.” The long way runs through the dark service tunnel inside the dam, where TINY's barks echo from somewhere behind, and past the visitor centre's vending machine. “They didn't pay. Of course they didn't pay. They're squirrels.”

A convoy of trucks cheers from the dam road. Night Owl: “They control the WATER now.” The crew spends the last night at the dam lookout. Far below are the lights of the city. Home.

Day 6: First Snow

The snow reaches the high pass in the morning. Dispatch doesn't bother with procedure: “The snow's on the pass. It'll reach the city tonight. ...Go. Go now.”

There's no choice of roads today. Buck drives the snowplow down the pass in person, and the jeep follows in its track: “Stay in my tracks and don't touch anything. ...Please.” The storm follows them down the mountain, and so does TINY, one last time.

Dispatch guides them through the city streets: “Left at the old mill, then straight down to the park. ...Hypothetically.” Gravy Train: “Proudest day of my life.” Night Owl: “They're going HOME. Under OUR parks.”

Home

The jeep rolls into Maple Park at dusk, and the colony pours out of the Old Oak. TINY limps in behind, far too tired to chase anything, and curls up under the oak. Nobody makes it leave. The deer watches from across the park.

On the radio, Acornucopia wants its cargo back. Dispatch: “Cargo... lost in transit. Write it off.”

How the evening ends depends on the crew:

  • If they beat the snow, the first snowflake lands on Maple Park just as the last golden acorn goes into the oak. “First snow's falling. ...Somewhere out there, a crate of golden acorns made it home. Stand down. Over.” And the feast begins.
  • If they were too slow, the Old Oak is already snowed in. The crew digs the front door out, and the feast happens anyway.
  • If every last acorn fell out on the way, the crate is empty and the colony stares. Then one golden acorn rolls out of a squirrel's cheek (the one it's been secretly chewing for six days), gets sliced two hundred ways, and is the best feast they ever had.

Buck parks the plow: “Best week of work we ever had. And we didn't do any of it.” Then, at last, Unit Seven calls in: “Dispatch, this is Unit Seven. Got a new radio. ...Did I miss anything?” And Dispatch says: “...No. Quiet week. Over.”

After the credits, there's one last whisper from Night Owl: “They got home. They have a car now. ...This isn't over.”

The running jokes

The deer is at every stop, and nobody ever mentions it.

Night Owl's theories grow every day: they're organised, then they have a navy, then they're building infrastructure, then they're in the rail network, then they control the water, and finally they're going home under our parks. Every theory is true.

Buck loses one more machine every day and slowly stops minding, because the squirrels keep doing the jobs on Buck's list.

Dispatch never stops calling “Unit Seven”. Unit Seven never answers, until the very end.

Everything Dispatch hears is small: small hammering, small grunting, a small scream, a smaller horn.
